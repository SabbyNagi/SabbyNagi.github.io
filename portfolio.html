Case 01

A consistent inbound delivery experience — matched to customer demand, across merchants and carriers.

Amazon · Inbound · Merchant + carrier facing
Gap
  1. Merchants churning out of the inbound managed-transportation subscription — low on-time pickup and low on-time delivery were driving opt-outs. A multi-billion-dollar SKU-breadth opportunity sat locked: merchants withheld SKUs they didn't trust to the network.
  2. Defect attribution was a black box — damage, shortage, late, refused: no closed-loop signal told you whether fault sat with the merchant, the carrier, or the warehouse. Disputes ran on phone calls, claims aged into weeks.
  3. Communication was reactive — disruptions surfaced only when the merchant called in. No proactive alerts, no entry architecture for merchant-side problem management.
  4. Capacity blindness at scheduling — merchants couldn't see warehouse capacity at appointment; driver check-in and unload dwell sat at hours-to-days against P90 targets of minutes.
  5. Manifest accuracy below 95% — broken ASN data cascaded into wrong-inventory, mispayments, and chargebacks.
Build
Built the Inbound Delivery Experience (IBDX) platform end-to-end. Three pillars — appointment scheduling, execution & delivery, disruption recovery — sit behind one surface used by both merchants and carriers, matched to downstream customer demand. Stood up a 3PL carrier program (scorecards, tiering, performance-tied volume) across the long tail of third-party carriers in the network — including 3P pallet, 3P floor, and Amazon Freight lanes — and a white-glove program for a select set of merchants: concierge onboarding, dedicated capacity, SLA escalation lane.
As a result
  1. ~1 wk → <24h cycle time, inventory setup → customer delivery.
  2. $X B merchant revenue (2-yr) — retention + SKU-breadth unlock.
  3. +6.2% on-time arrival, lift to on-time pickup.
  4. 95% manifest accuracy — ASN chargebacks closed.
  5. −49% / −34% / −30% P90 live-unload (1P / 3P pallet / 3P floor).
  6. −5.7% Amazon-caused reschedules — defect attribution moved upstream.
Case 02

On-time pickup matched to capacity — a three-year roadmap to AI-driven scheduling that aligns capacity with demand.

Amazon · First-mile · AI roadmap
Gap
  1. Freight-ready dates ran on static SLA tables — disconnected from real carrier and warehouse capacity. Every published date was a guess; merchant trust in the inbound network eroded.
  2. Cascade of infeasibility — bad FRD → carrier no-show → reschedule → wasted dock → storefront out-of-stock. Repositioning miles burned, claims aged.
  3. Defect attribution was downstream-only — disruptions surfaced after pickup failure; no upstream signal that the date was infeasible at commit. Every miss became a claim, not a prevention.
  4. Scheduling was deterministic — manual rules, no learning loop, no autonomous mitigation; the engine couldn't get smarter over time.
Build
Built the FRD engine to match on-time pickup commitments to real network capacity — merchants see only feasible dates, the network commits only to dates it can keep. Shipped a carrier API so transportation partners read feasibility and write tender / status back into the platform. Then sequenced a three-year roadmap: deterministic rules → ML-augmented forecasting → AI-autonomous scheduling that aligns capacity with demand.
As a result
  1. ↑0.8–5% on-time pickup across 4 modes (TL, AF LTL, 3P LTL, intermodal).
  2. ↓X% .com out-of-stocks → $X incremental sales — on-time pickup → on-shelf availability.
  3. ↑X% capacity utilization — every missed pickup is a wasted dock + stranded trailer; FRDs unlock capacity without capex.
  4. Preferred slots for top-tier merchants — capacity tied to scorecard, self-reinforcing with the 3PL carrier program.
  5. $X annual cost savings — reschedules down, dock recovered, miles cut.
Case 03

A consistent delivery experience across every customer touchpoint.

Chewy · Customer-facing
Gap
  1. Carrier scans, warehouse manifests, address quality, and EDD/PDD signals all lived in separate systems.
  2. Estimated Delivery Date was the customer's promise — and it was wrong. EDD ran on static transit tables, blind to live carrier scans, weather, and capacity, so the date on the order page didn't match what the network would actually do.
  3. Weather-related disruptions surfaced after the miss — no upstream signal flagged at-risk shipments before the customer called.
  4. ~2% of orders had invalid addresses — re-routes drove carrier surcharges and a steady stream of "Where is my order?" contacts.
  5. Zone-skipping handoff between national and regional carriers was a line-haul blind spot.
Build
Built WIZMO as the unified event layer behind every customer surface — address validation, a live EDD/PDD engine, weather-signal ingestion, and carrier tracking writing to one canonical timeline. Every customer surface — tracking page, proactive email, contact center, order detail — reads the same answer. Weather and capacity disruptions now surface upstream and re-quote EDD before the customer hits refresh.
As a result
  1. ↑X pts customer NPS / CSAT — EDD accuracy + proactive weather re-quote.
  2. ↑X% EDD accuracy — live carrier, capacity, weather signals replaced static tables.
  3. ↑3% on-time delivery — order-entry address validation caught bad shipments pre-carrier.
  4. ↓60% WIZMO contact volume — one timeline replaced cross-carrier reconciliation.
  5. Weather flags moved from post-miss to pre-miss — at-risk shipments caught before EDD broke.
